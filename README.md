# Multiplex Screen Scheduling — a four-model Operations Research pipeline

**Operations Research project — Group 14**
Abhishek Karthik (CB.SC.U4AIE23010) · Abhishek R (CB.SC.U4AIE23011) · M P Mothiswaran (CB.SC.U4AIE23041) · M S Bavesh Reddy (CB.SC.U4AIE23044)

A decision-support tool that builds a feasible one-day operating plan for a 5-screen multiplex from real TMDB movie metadata and a transparent synthetic theatre configuration. It tests whether the plan beats a simple popularity-based schedule, and it **puts a price on every trade-off**: the cost of balancing goals, of the variety policy, and of the budget.

It comes in two forms that share one implementation (`engine/`):

* **The report notebook** (`multiplex_screen_scheduling.ipynb`): formulations, results, sensitivity, robustness and conclusions, with every number generated by code.
* **The interactive app** (`app/`, Streamlit):
  * load the data and generate statistics on demand;
  * run any of the four optimisations, each showing its formulation, its input data, the exact model sent to the solver, the solution and its sensitivity information;
  * compare against the baselines, run what-if and sensitivity experiments, and export the final plan.

## The model chain

| # | Model | OR technique | Decides | Output → consumed by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Film selection | 0/1 knapsack (binary ILP) | which films to license under the ₹20 L weekly budget (8–12 films, content coverage, only films that fit a slot) | licensed films → Models 2, 3, 4 |
| 2 | Screen–genre capacity planning | Transportation LP (balanced; spill + empty-seat cost; duals and sensitivity report) | how many shows of each genre go on each screen | quota `x[s,g]` → Model 4 goal G6; preferred genre per screen → Model 3 constraint C7 |
| 3 | Film–screen–slot assignment | Binary ILP | revenue-maximising 5 × 6 timetable (one film per cell, runtime + cleanup fit, no simulcast, ≤ 5 shows per film, ≥ 4 screens per slot, every licensed film shown) | optimal revenue R\* → Model 4 goal G1 |
| 4 | Multi-objective reconciliation | Weighted goal programming (MILP), weights by **AHP** (consistency ratio 0.004) | final schedule balancing revenue, occupancy, prime-time variety, idle time and the Model 2 plan | **final schedule** + goal-attainment report |

**Evaluation:**
- a hard-constraint validator for every schedule;
- two baselines (B1 naive popularity, B2 greedy manager);
- a revenue bridge (selection vs scheduling effect);
- Solver-style LP sensitivity reports for Models 1 and 2;
- end-to-end sensitivity on λ, budget, demand scale and the variety policy;
- a goal-programming trade-off curve;
- a robustness test over 10 rule-selected weeks.

**Main-week results (16 Jul 2014):**
- Final plan: ₹4,16,230/day, **+61.8%** against the naive schedule.
- The revenue optimum R\* is ₹4,19,711, so the price of balance is **0.83%**.
- The 8-film variety policy costs ₹44,251/day.
- Weekly ticket revenue covers 1.46× the licensing spend.
- The final plan wins in 9 of 10 test weeks.

## Changing the scenario

* **In the app:** use the sidebar (week, λ, demand scale, screens, prices, slot factors) and the controls on each model page (budget, film limits, fee formula, quota rule, show limits, AHP judgments or manual weights).
* **In code:** every assumption lives in `engine/config.py`.
  * `run_chain(clean, cfg)` returns every intermediate result.
  * `run_pipeline({'budget': 2_500_000}, clean=clean)` returns a summary row.
  * `sweep(clean, 'lam', [0.35, 0.55, 0.75])` re-solves the whole chain for each value.

## Key modelling assumptions

- **Slots:** six fixed 180-minute slots: 09:00, 12:00, 15:00, 18:00, 21:00 and 00:00. With a 20-minute cleanup, a film must be ≤ 160 minutes long.
- **Demand in persons:** `D = DEMAND_SCALE × effective_demand × slot_factor × genre_slot_affinity`. Seats sold = `min(D, screen capacity)`; the excess is spill.
  - `effective_demand = normalised popularity × exp(−λ · weeks since release)`, with λ = 0.55 (assumed; swept 0.25–0.85).
  - `DEMAND_SCALE = 650`, calibrated once to an average occupancy of 35–45%, then frozen.
- **Licensing fee:** a weekly minimum guarantee of ₹1 L + ₹12.5 L × effective demand (rounded to ₹500); budget ₹20 L.
  - These are half the first values, which changes no decision (verified) and makes weekly ticket revenue cover the licensing spend.
- **Model 2 quotas:** each licensed film gets 1–5 shows in proportion to its demand; the quotas are summed per genre.
- **Goal weights:** from AHP pairwise comparisons declared in `engine/config.py`. Targets are one step better than the revenue-only schedule, so the goals genuinely conflict.

All revenue and occupancy figures are model estimates built on these declared assumptions, not observed ticket sales. Limitations and future work are discussed in Section 15 of the notebook; the post-review corrections, with computed before/after numbers, are in Section 14.
